Déjà Vu at the Plant Nursery

3 October 2026 · Jennifer Navis · 7 min read

Do you believe in karma? What goes around comes around? Or the saying that history repeats itself? Or that you'll experience the same thing over and over until you finally learn something from it?

I've always wondered how much truth there is to these sayings. I mean, does life really have a way of teaching us lessons, or do we just happen to notice certain things when we've experienced something similar before?

Anyway, something happened recently that got me thinking about all this.

It was 9.30 a.m., and I was running late for work. I had a lot to finish that day, and some of it was rather important. So I hurried to the office, mentally preparing myself for the workload, only to discover that there was no electricity.

Apparently, there was maintenance work going on in the area, and the UPS had exhausted itself after running throughout the night.

Wonderful!

I was disappointed, obviously. It was a crucial day, and I couldn't get anything done. But somewhere deep inside, I was actually quite happy.

An unexpected day off!

Oh, the joy!

I hadn't had much time for myself in almost three months. Work had been keeping me ridiculously busy, and there were so many things I'd wanted to do but couldn't find the time for. I won't get into the details because that's a story for another day.

Anyway, it was past 11 a.m. when I reached home.

My mother thought I'd be skipping lunch as usual because I'm currently experimenting with yet another diet.

Yes. Another one.

I've tried different diets to lose that extra flab, but unfortunately, the flab seems rather attached to me. If anything, it's growing.

And the worst part?

I'm a foodie.

A foodie on a diet.

My eyes can barely tolerate reading that sentence. Imagine how my soul feels having to live it.

So, instead of giving up the food I love, I decided to skip a meal. Lunch, specifically. I'd somehow convinced myself that our ancestors managed without eating three meals a day, so perhaps skipping one wouldn't make much difference.

Of course, that's something I told myself, not a conclusion based on any serious research.

It's a lovely theory when your belly is full. By 5 p.m., however, I'd be so hungry that I wouldn't hesitate to swallow an entire pig.

Anyway, back to my unexpected holiday.

I was ridiculously excited because I finally had time to do all the things I'd been putting off.

Should I read a book? Write a journal? Draw something? Paint my nails? Go shopping? Catch up on all the anime I'd missed? Watch a movie? Play a game? Lie on the couch and scroll through reels? Or simply catch up on all the sleep I'd lost?

Oh, sleep!

That sounded tempting.

But I couldn't possibly waste such a precious day sleeping, could I?

So I sat there thinking about what to do, and before I knew it, almost an hour had passed.

An entire hour deciding how to spend my free time.

How ridiculous is that? I'd been waiting months for a day to myself, and now that I finally had one, I didn't know what to do with it.

It was past noon when I remembered a notepad I'd kept in one of the drawers. I went looking for it, couldn't find it, and decided to check the cabinet underneath.

And that's when I discovered a huge bag filled with pens and pencils.

I'd dumped the whole collection into that cabinet when we moved into the apartment two years ago. Every time I cleaned it, I'd remove the bag, wipe the shelves and put everything back exactly where it had been.

But not today.

For some reason, I decided that organising those pens and pencils was suddenly the most important thing in the world.

So I emptied the bag, checked which pens worked, separated the pencils and arranged everything neatly into holders.

Quite satisfying, actually.

Until I looked at the clock.

5.30 p.m.

FIVE THIRTY!

I'd spent my entire unexpected holiday practically organising stationery.

Of all the things I could've done!

Well, at least the holders looked nice.

Now, it was time for plant shopping.

Let me tell you something about myself. I have this habit of becoming obsessed with things. A couple of years ago, it was plants. Last year, it was fish. And this year, apparently, plants again.

So one thing I've learnt about myself is that I can become obsessed with the same thing more than once.

Interesting discovery, isn't it?

Anyway, I went to a nursery that happened to be located right in the middle of a food street, next to a shawarma shop.

Now imagine this.

I'd skipped lunch. I was hungry. I love food. And there was shawarma.

A rather dangerous combination, don't you think?

All that determination I'd shown throughout the afternoon disappeared the moment I saw the food.

I devoured that shawarma like there was no tomorrow.

And honestly, I regret nothing.

After satisfying my stomach, I finally entered the nursery and selected a few plants and pots. It was already around 8 p.m., and I wasn't particularly interested in visiting another nursery. Besides, I'd spent enough time choosing everything, so I wanted to finish shopping and go home.

There was a young boy there, probably fourteen or fifteen, who began calculating the prices.

He pressed the buttons on his calculator repeatedly, looked at the total and announced:

The boy: ₹3,500.

Me: ₹2,500.

The boy: No. ₹3,500.

Me: ₹2,700.

The boy: No. ₹3,500.

Me: I'm buying quite a few things. Reduce the price a little. ₹2,800.

He went back to his calculator, pressed a few buttons and looked up.

The boy: Okay. ₹3,450.

₹3,450?

A discount of fifty rupees?

After all that bargaining?

I was getting annoyed.

Me: That's not how you negotiate.

The boy: I have to put the plants into the pots. I'm not even charging you for the mud and cocopeat. You're only paying for the plants.

Me: Fine. I'll pay for the mud and cocopeat too. ₹3,000. That's my final offer. You accept, or I leave.

I turned around and started walking towards the exit.

The boy: Okay. ₹3,000.

Finally!

He gathered the plants and pots and took them away to begin potting them.

A few minutes later, he returned, clicking his calculator again.

Oh, for heaven's sake.

The boy: I forgot to add two items. The total comes to ₹3,700.

Me: Okay. ₹3,200.

The boy: No way. I can't lose that much.

Me: You originally said ₹3,500, and I offered ₹3,000. Now you're saying ₹3,700, so I'm offering ₹3,200. It's still the same difference of ₹500.

The boy: Not happening.

Me: Fine. I'll buy them somewhere else.

I started walking towards the exit again.

The boy: Okay.

And that was it.

I came home empty-handed.

I was irritated, of course. I'd spent all that time selecting plants, choosing pots and negotiating prices, only to return home without buying anything. I thought the boy was rude and arrogant and had quite a few unpleasant things to say about him.

To myself, of course.

Later that night, I was still thinking about the incident.

Why wouldn't he negotiate? Wouldn't it have been better to make the sale rather than lose a customer over a few hundred rupees?

And then, somewhere in the middle of my thoughts, I remembered something.

I used to do that.

When I was relatively new to business, I sometimes refused to negotiate because I didn't want to lose a few thousand rupees.

I thought I was protecting my business. And sometimes, I probably was. After all, there's no point accepting every order if you're going to end up losing money.

But there were also times when I was so focused on saving a few thousand rupees that I failed to consider the bigger picture. I lost opportunities worth lakhs because I didn't know when to negotiate and when to stand firm.

It took me a while to understand the difference.

And here I was, getting annoyed with a young boy whose behaviour reminded me of my own.

Of course, I don't know whether he was allowed to reduce the price. Maybe he had instructions to follow. Maybe he genuinely couldn't offer anything lower. I was looking at the situation from a customer's perspective, and he had his own reasons.

But it was funny how the whole incident brought back memories of my early days in business.

I went out to buy plants and ended up thinking about the mistakes I'd made while learning to run a business.

Was it karma? History repeating itself? Or was I simply reminded of something because I'd experienced it before?

I don't know.

But later that night, when I thought about the boy again, I smiled.

I suppose experience has a funny way of changing how we look at things.

Anyway, I went plant shopping and came home with something to write about instead.

Still no plants, though.
