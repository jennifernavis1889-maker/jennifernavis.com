The ₹500 Lesson

3 October 2026 · Jennifer Navis · 7 min read

Do you believe in karma? What goes around comes around? Or the saying that history repeats itself? Or that you'll keep experiencing the same thing over and over until you learn something from it?

I don't know how much truth there is to any of these, but something happened recently that got me thinking.

It was 9.30 a.m., and I was running late for work. I had a lot to finish that day, and some of it was rather important, so I hurried to the office only to discover that there was no power.

Apparently, there was maintenance work going on in the area, and the UPS had already exhausted itself after working throughout the night.

Wonderful.

I was disappointed because I had planned to finish quite a lot of work that day. But somewhere deep inside, I was also rather pleased.

An unexpected day off!

I hadn't had much time for myself in almost three months, and suddenly I had an entire day to do whatever I wanted.

It was past 11 a.m. when I reached home. My mother thought I'd be skipping lunch as usual because I'm currently experimenting with another diet.

Yes, another diet.

I've tried different diets to lose the extra flab, but unfortunately, the flab doesn't seem particularly interested in leaving me. If anything, it's growing.

And the worst part is that I absolutely love food.

A foodie on a diet.

My eyes can barely tolerate reading that sentence. Imagine how my soul feels having to live it.

So, instead of giving up the food I love, I decided to skip a meal. Lunch, specifically.

I'd even managed to convince myself that our ancestors probably didn't eat three meals a day, so perhaps skipping one wasn't such a terrible idea.

Of course, this was something I told myself. I hadn't exactly conducted historical or nutritional research.

It's a lovely theory when your belly is full. By 5 p.m., however, I'd be so hungry that I wouldn't hesitate to swallow an entire pig.

Anyway, back to my unexpected holiday.

I was excited because there were so many things I'd wanted to do over the past three months. Read a book, write something, draw, paint my nails, go shopping, catch up on the anime I'd missed, watch a movie, play a game, scroll through reels or simply sleep.

Oh, sleep!

That sounded rather tempting.

But I couldn't possibly waste this precious opportunity sleeping, could I?

So I spent the next hour deciding what to do.

Yes. An entire hour.

I had finally found some free time, and I was spending it wondering how to spend it.

It was past noon when I remembered a notepad I'd kept in one of the drawers. I went looking for it, couldn't find it, and decided to check the cabinet underneath.

That's when I discovered a huge bag filled with pens and pencils.

I'd dumped it there when we moved into the apartment two years ago. Every time I cleaned that cabinet, I'd remove the bag, wipe the shelves and put everything back exactly where it had been.

But this time, for reasons unknown even to me, I decided to organise the entire collection.

I sorted the pens, checked which ones worked, separated the pencils and arranged everything neatly into holders.

Quite satisfying, actually.

Until I looked at the clock.

5.30 p.m.

I'd spent nearly my entire day off organising stationery.

I had books waiting to be read, things waiting to be written and an impressive amount of anime to catch up on, but apparently, the pens and pencils were more important.

Well, at least I accomplished something.

Now, it was time for plant shopping.

One thing I've learnt about myself is that I tend to become obsessed with things. A couple of years ago, it was plants. Last year, fish. And now, plants again.

Apparently, I can become obsessed with the same thing more than once.

Anyway, I went to a nursery that happened to be located in the middle of a food street, right next to a shawarma shop.

Now imagine this.

I'd skipped lunch. I was hungry. I love food. And there was shawarma.

What did I expect would happen?

I devoured it like there was no tomorrow.

So much for my diet.

After satisfying my stomach, I finally entered the nursery and selected a few plants and pots.

It was already around 8 p.m., and I wasn't in the mood to visit another nursery. Besides, I'd spent enough time choosing everything, so I wanted to finish the shopping and go home.

There was a boy there, probably around fourteen or fifteen, who began calculating the prices.

He pressed the buttons on his calculator repeatedly, looked at the total and announced:

The boy: ₹3,500.

Me: ₹2,500.

The boy: No. ₹3,500.

Me: ₹2,700.

The boy: No. ₹3,500.

Me: I'm buying quite a few things. Reduce the price a little. ₹2,800.

He calculated again.

The boy: Okay. ₹3,450.

Fifty rupees?

After all that bargaining?

I was getting annoyed.

Me: That's not how you negotiate.

The boy: I have to put the plants into the pots. I'm not even charging you for the mud and cocopeat. You're only paying for the plants.

Me: Fine. I'll pay for the mud and cocopeat. ₹3,000. That's my final offer. You accept, or I leave.

I turned around and started walking towards the exit.

The boy: Okay. ₹3,000.

Finally!

He gathered the plants and pots and took them away to begin potting them.

A few minutes later, he came back with his calculator.

The boy: I forgot to add two items. The total is ₹3,700.

Oh, for heaven's sake.

Me: Okay. ₹3,200.

The boy: No. I can't lose that much.

Me: You originally said ₹3,500, and I offered ₹3,000. Now you're saying ₹3,700, so I'm offering ₹3,200. It's still the same difference of ₹500.

The boy: Not happening.

Me: Fine. I'll buy them somewhere else.

I started walking towards the exit again.

The boy: Okay.

And that was it.

No plants.

I was irritated. I'd spent so much time selecting everything, negotiating over the price and waiting around, only to return home empty-handed.

I thought he was rude and arrogant, and I had a few choice words for him in my head. Thankfully, they stayed there.

Later that night, I was still thinking about the whole thing.

Why wouldn't he negotiate? Wouldn't it have been better to make the sale rather than lose a customer over a few hundred rupees?

And that's when I remembered something.

I used to do that.

When I was relatively new to business, there were times when I'd refuse to negotiate because I didn't want to lose a few thousand rupees.

I thought I was protecting my business.

Sometimes I was.

But there were also times when I was so focused on the immediate amount that I failed to consider the business I might receive from that customer in the future.

I ended up losing opportunities worth lakhs while trying to save a few thousand.

It took experience to understand the difference between protecting my margins and being unnecessarily rigid.

And here was this young boy, standing behind a counter, reminding me of those days.

Of course, I don't know whether he was allowed to reduce the price any further. Perhaps he had his reasons. I was looking at the situation from a customer's perspective, just as he was looking at it from his side of the counter.

Still, the resemblance amused me.

I had gone to buy plants and ended up remembering something about my early days in business.

Was that karma? History repeating itself? Or simply a coincidence that reminded me of an old lesson?

I don't know.

But I smiled when I thought about that boy again.

He's young, and he has plenty of time to learn. Just as I did.

And as for me, I suppose the experience was a useful reminder.

Not that I'd forgotten everything I'd learnt.

Sometimes it's simply interesting to see something familiar from the other side of the counter.

Anyway, I still haven't bought my plants.
